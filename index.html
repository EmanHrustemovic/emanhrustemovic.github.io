Published Research

Sentiment Analysis on 568,000+ Reviews

The Problem

We wanted to show how comments under an article actually affect whether someone reading it, as a future buyer, decides to purchase.

What I Did

I handled the ML side of the research — the research questions, the models, the paper — under my professor's supervision. The hardest part was class imbalance: one class (5-star) dominated the data. We tackled it with a plain baseline first, then SMOTE, then class weighting, comparing results across all three instead of picking one blind.

What Came Of It

Raw text alone wasn't enough to predict outcomes reliably — that was the real finding, and it's a more honest one than pretending a single model solved it cleanly.

Published, International Burch University — Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina
FlyRank ML Internship

Content Decline Scoring

The Problem

FlyRank needed a way to automatically flag which pages were losing search ranking, instead of someone manually reviewing thousands of pages by hand.

What I Did

I framed one page-month as one row, then built a simple baseline first: flag a page if its click-through rate was far below what its search position would predict. Before trusting that, I tested it against two signals — content staleness turned out useless, while CTR-vs-position held up. I trained a Logistic Regression model on top of that, then re-ran everything under a stricter, client-grouped split to make sure I wasn't fooling myself.

What Came Of It

The model beat the simple rule — Precision@50 went from 0.70 to 0.96, meaning almost every page it flagged as declining actually was. The hardest part wasn't the code, it was trusting which model was actually the right one instead of the one I assumed would work going in.
